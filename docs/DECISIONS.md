# Founding Decisions

These decisions are settled for the founding repository. A revisit trigger opens discussion; it does not silently change the decision. Material changes require the governance process and, where applicable, an RFC and ADR.

| ID | Decision | Status | Rationale | Consequence | Revisit trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FD-001 | Use Apache License 2.0, with no bootstrap CLA or copyleft licence. | Locked | Broad use plus an explicit patent grant. | Contributions use Apache 2.0 and DCO sign-off. | A documented legal risk or ecosystem incompatibility. |
| FD-002 | Use the dedicated `nz-ledger` organisation owned through `@nz365guy`, with a trusted backup owner. | Locked | Independent identity and ownership continuity without shared credentials. | No separate project login; owners retain recovery responsibility. | A mature governance structure replaces founder-led stewardship. |
| FD-003 | Start with one public monorepo, `nz-ledger/nz-ledger`. | Locked | Keeps contracts, implementation, tests, and governance coherent. | Split only for a real independent lifecycle or boundary. | Independent release, maintainer, security, deployment, or dependency boundary. |
| FD-004 | Use TypeScript for the future reference implementation, backed by language-neutral schemas and conformance. | Locked | Practical reference implementation without making the platform language-specific. | Other languages must prove equivalent behaviour. | Conformance evidence shows TypeScript blocks a core property. |
| FD-005 | Begin as a modular monolith with PostgreSQL, versioned evidence storage, and a transactional outbox. | Locked | Minimises distributed complexity while preserving modular boundaries and durable integration. | No microservices or universal event sourcing at launch. | Operational evidence demonstrates a genuine independent boundary. |
| FD-006 | Keep the accounting kernel deterministic and headless. | Locked | Reproducibility is a financial control. | Time, identifiers, rates, and other nondeterministic inputs are explicit. | Never for convenience; only a formally reviewed correctness requirement. |
| FD-007 | Posted journals are immutable and corrected by linked reversing or adjusting journals. | Locked | Preserves history and evidence. | A journal is not edited, deleted, or changed to a `reversed` state. | No planned revisit. |
| FD-008 | Represent financial values exactly and serialise public financial values as decimal strings. | Locked | Floating point cannot preserve required financial exactness. | Coefficient and scale, or an equivalent exact representation, is mandatory. | A more rigorous exact model proves compatible with public contracts. |
| FD-009 | Treat multicurrency and multiple financial instruments as foundational. | Locked | Quantity, settlement, valuation, and functional-currency effects are distinct. | Journals balance in functional currency while retaining other quantities and valuations. | No planned revisit. |
| FD-010 | Model stable-value and digital instruments as financial instruments, not ordinary fiat by reference. | Locked | Issuer, jurisdiction, redemption, network, custody, valuation, tax, and risk differ. | Record and reconcile only; no private-key custody at launch. | Legal, accounting, and security review supports a changed boundary. |
| FD-011 | Implement changing New Zealand rules through versioned, effective-dated policy packs. | Locked | Historical outputs must remain reproducible and source-backed. | Policy changes never silently alter filed or closed periods. | A reviewed model offers stronger traceability and compatibility. |
| FD-012 | Treat original evidence and provenance as first-class assets. | Locked | Derived and AI-extracted data cannot replace source evidence. | Traceability spans evidence, events, subledgers, journals, reports, and outputs. | No planned revisit. |
| FD-013 | Preserve effective time and recorded time. | Locked | Current-corrected and previously-known positions are both required. | Filing and close snapshots retain their historical basis. | No planned revisit. |
| FD-014 | Give humans, agents, integrations, and scheduled rules the same controlled command surface. | Locked | Actor type must not bypass authority or audit. | Every principal has explicit mandate, scope, limits, evidence, approvals, and expiry. | New actor types require equivalent or stricter controls. |
| FD-015 | Extend through public contracts and isolated permissions. | Locked | An ecosystem must not bypass invariants or expose excess data. | No direct core-storage writes or arbitrary code inside the kernel. | A reviewed extension boundary proves stronger isolation. |
| FD-016 | Treat financial integrity, tenant isolation, authority, policy, evidence, valuation, and release-chain failures as security issues. | Locked | Security includes correctness and accountability, not only confidentiality. | Security review covers accounting and supply-chain invariants. | No planned revisit. |
| FD-017 | Avoid premature implementation complexity. | Locked | Speculation increases cost and weakens auditability. | No premature microservices, rule language, marketplace, custody, extra languages, or empty packages. | Accepted evidence-backed issue and architecture decision. |
| FD-018 | Native digital signing: signature, digest, signer-identity, and verification semantics are first-class public contracts from Phase 1. Signing and verification execute in a platform service outside the deterministic kernel; the kernel stores and validates signatures as explicit input. Key custody is pluggable (HSM, cloud KMS, or local keys) and never lives inside the kernel. | Locked | Signed financial records must be native to the contracts, not retrofitted, while FD-006 forbids nondeterministic operations such as key use inside the kernel. | Phase 1 contract design includes signature envelopes over canonical bytes. No kernel dependency on cryptographic key material. | A reviewed design proves a stronger placement for signing semantics. |
| FD-019 | Posted history is tamper-evident, not only immutable: each book's accounting sequence forms an append-only hash chain in which every posted journal's canonical digest incorporates the previous entry's digest, so non-mutation is cryptographically verifiable from an export without trusting the operator or storage. | Locked | Immutability enforced only at the application layer must be trusted; a hash chain makes it independently verifiable by human and AI auditors. | Chain construction depends on the canonical-serialisation contract (issue #6) and is specified with it. Verification is a required export and conformance capability. | A reviewed design proves an equivalently verifiable, simpler mechanism. |
| FD-020 | No blockchain or distributed-ledger journal store. Integrity properties are delivered by exact values, immutable posted history, the FD-019 hash chain, FD-018 signing, and deterministic replay under a single accountable operator. Assets that exist on external blockchains are recorded as financial instruments under FD-010. | Locked | A legal entity's books have an accountable operator by law; distributed consensus solves a trust problem this system does not have, while adding cost, latency, privacy conflicts, and key-loss risk. | The platform never requires a blockchain to operate or to prove integrity. | A reviewed module proposal for optional external anchoring of chain-head digests to an independent timestamping authority, as third-party proof of existence. Anchoring, if ever accepted, is a module, not core. |
| FD-021 | Access surfaces are peer adapters. The HTTP API, CLI, MCP server, and any future transport are stateless adapters over the command and intent gateway. Adapters contain no business logic, evaluate no authority, and hold no financial state, and every reference adapter must pass the same black-box conformance vectors as the gateway. Adapter-specific behaviour is limited to transport concerns such as authentication binding, session handling, and serialisation framing. | Locked | A convenience path in one surface would silently bypass authority, audit, or kernel invariants and split behaviour between surfaces that must remain identical. | No surface exposes a capability the public gateway contracts cannot express. Reference adapters ship with adapter-level conformance evidence. | A reviewed design proves a genuine capability cannot be delivered as an adapter over the gateway. |
